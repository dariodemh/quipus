# s1 fuentes (resumen; el informe completo esta en el mensaje final)

1. Urton 2005 LAA 16(2):147-167, doi 10.2307/30042809 (portada Harvard DASH). Abstract p.147: "Each community in the empire had a minimum of four khipu accountants, all of whom are said by the chronicler Garcilaso de la Vega to have kept the same records. This study examines several examples of matching khipu accounts identified among sets of two or three khipu." Pares: AS194/AS195 (AMNH 41.2/6702-6703, Callango, Ica; p.149); AS159/AS173 (Berlin VA16138A / ...; Ica-Pisco; p.150-151); AS114/AS123 (Berlin VA16148 / VA47085; p.152); AS116/AS150 (Berlin VA47125 / VA47109, Ica; p.156); Puruchuco (Mackey Nos. 2,3,9; p.155-157, "Three-Way Khipu Matches"); Chachapoyas UR6/UR9/UR21 (pp.158-164). Conclusion (p.~163): "Garcilaso's observations ... can now be confirmed, at least at a general level." No mention of AS38, AS84, HP055, UR1104, UR234, UR122. Todos los pares AS estan dentro de un mismo museo (Berlin) salvo AS194/195 (AMNH, ambos).
2. Florio & Overmann 2025 JoCC 25:128-158. Abstract p.128: "questioning its inclusion of a concept of zero and its interpretation as a positional system. We conclude that Inka numbers and computational methods were likely non-positional". p.136: "the blank space on a string (meaning no value is present) resembles the blank space used to align Mesopotamian positional numbers (a precursor of zero)"; "Certainly, the blank space suggests the concept of positionality had the potential to emerge ... Nonetheless, the blank space represented a compromise between the need to tie clusters of knots along strings and the impulse to use knots that were simple ... It is merely a consequence of using string and knots as the representing medium." p.137: "if the Inkas had conceived of zero as a number, they most likely would have indicated it with a particular knot"; "The lack of any knot for zero ... suggest the khipu was an additive system." p.135: identical knots "would be used in an additive system, where a zero is unneeded".
