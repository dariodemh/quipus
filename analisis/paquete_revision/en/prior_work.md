# Prior work on the findings marked as new

For each finding in `tabla_hallazgos.csv` marked as new (or as partly published, in whatever part is new), we give:

- the closest published work we know of;
- why it does not contain the finding;
- how much we trust that search.

**Sources of the search:**

- `analisis/ds/ant_incahuasi/` (H1–H7): OpenAlex, 25 works citing Urton and Chu, supplements and full texts of Urton 2017 (ch. 9), Urton and Chu 2015 and 2018, and Urton and Brezine 2005.
- `analisis/ds/ant_resto/` (R1–R9): OpenAlex, Zenodo, GitHub and PDF.
- Direct collation by the coordinator against the texts in `analisis/fuentes_txt/`.

**Confidence of the search:**

- **High:** the full text of the closest source was read.
- **Medium:** abstracts, supplements or excerpts were read.
- **Low:** no search specific to that finding was carried out. In that case, «new» only means that it does not appear in our 57 reading cards (*fichas*) or in the project's state of the art. **A specialist may know prior work that we have missed.**

**General caveat for Inkawasi.** We have not been able to read:

- the article by Urton and Chu on Academia.edu, «El archivo khipu de Incawasi…»;
- Chu 2016 (Actas del I Congreso Nacional de Arqueología) nor the two 2018 articles by Chu in *Boletín Yungas*;
- the body of Covey and Dalton 2025.

## Inkawasi

### I01. UR270 (pendant cord + 1st subsidiary cord) = cells of UR278

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2015, supplement (appendix I, p. 2), and Urton 2017, appendix (p. 178): «UR278: Cords organized in sets of 9 cords. Values primarily in low three-digits (100s–500s)». Urton and Chu 2015 (pp. 520–522) state that UR269, UR270 and UR272 «display a repeating numerical value».
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - UR278 is described as a table, without relating it to any other khipu;
  - UR270 appears only in the inventory and in that sentence about repeated values;
  - no work citing Urton and Chu mentions UR270 or UR278 with figures (0 of 25).
- **Confidence:** high (ch. 9 in full, the 2015 and 2018 supplements, the 2018 article in English and in Spanish).

### I02. Row totals of UR278 → headers of UR275/UR266, in order

- **Closest:**
  - Urton 2017, ch. 9: UR266 and UR275 as copies (18 exact and 8 close matches, pp. 166–167) and two loose sums between them (1070 + 1217 = 2287; 2106 + 1153 = 1653 + 1606 = 3259).
  - Urton and Brezine 2005, and Urton 2017 ch. 5: three-level hierarchy at Puruchuco.
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - Urton knows the totals of the books (6 of the 8 headers of H1 appear in 2018), but does not link them to the rows of UR278;
  - ch. 9 lists the Inkawasi techniques as sums *within* each khipu, copies and tied pairs;
  - the only sum *between* khipus that he cites is the one from Puruchuco (p. 165);
  - Galindo Zúñiga 2026 attributes «the relations between records» to Puruchuco, not to Inkawasi.
- **Confidence:** high.

### I03–I04. UR273A + UR273B → headers; UR274A replaces UR273A

- **Closest:** the 2015 supplement and the appendix of Urton 2017 (p. 177): «UR273A: All three-digit numbers (100s) and blanks. UR273B: Mostly four-digit number (1000s) and blanks. #s similar to 1000s numbers on matching pair UR266 & UR275». For UR274A and UR274B they say only «Repetition of fixed value '30'» and «'17'».
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - Urton saw that the thousands of UR273B *resemble* those of the books, but did not add the hundreds of UR273A;
  - of the 16 addends of the 8 exact sums, none appears in Urton 2017 or in the 2018 supplement as a loose number;
  - of the 8 headers, 6 do appear;
  - nobody relates UR274A to the empty records of UR273A.
- **Classification:** new, with an explicit qualitative antecedent. The 2015 supplement must be cited.
- **Confidence:** high.

### I05. UR267A + UR267B → UR269 (withdrawn)

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2018 (tables of UR267A and UR267B) and Urton 2017, ch. 9 (tied pairs and X/fixed/Y versus X/Y/fixed paradigms).
- **Why it does not contain it:** they publish the *internal* arithmetic of each half, not its sum towards UR269.
- Recorded only for the record: the relation did not pass the out-of-sample tests and is **not** claimed.

### I07. Levy 46 and levies that add up (30 + 17 = 47; 15 + 10 = 25), in whatever part is new

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2018 (pp. 7–8): «We have registered four different fixed values on the Inkawasi khipus: 10, 15, 47, and 208» and «There is some evidence to support the use of two additional fixed (taxation) values … 17 and 30. However, nowhere … do they display a consistent enough record…».
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - they know of 17 and 30, but discard them as fixed values and do not relate them to 47;
  - 46 does not appear in any accessible source.
- **What remains ours:** the hypothesis that the levies add up, which is proposed, and the 46.
- **Confidence:** medium (body of 2018 read; some sentences truncated in the JSTOR excerpt).

### I08. The levy is absolute (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2018 interpret the levy as a tax of about 2% of the deposit (UR275: 47 / 2257.8 = 2.1%).
- **Why it does not contain it:** they give the rate as an average and do not examine its dispersion by record, which runs from 1.1% to 4.2%. The fiscal interpretation is theirs; ours is only the precision that the levy is fixed and not proportional.
- **Confidence:** high.

### I09–I13. The 9 columns: shared identity with UR270, flatness, ordered profile, neither demography nor time, predictive power

- **Closest:** the 2015 supplement («sets of 9 cords») and Urton and Chu 2015, summary (p. 512): «possible standardized accounting units», based on the 23 × 23 cm grids of the floor.
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - the structure of 9 is published as a description;
  - the CV, the ordering by size, the correspondence with UR270, the comparison with demographic or seasonal models, and the prediction of rows are not published.
- **Confidence:** high for Urton and Chu; low for literature outside Inkawasi on «tables of 9».

### I14. Resemblance of the profile to the 9 pachacas (decimal administrative units) of Bambamarca

- **Closest:** Pärssinen 1992, *Tawantinsuyu*, table 2 (*visita* (colonial inspection) of González de Cuenca, 1567), and Espinoza Soriano 1972, «El reino de Cuismanco» (list of guarangas (units of a thousand) and pachacas of Cajamarca).
- **Why it does not contain it:** these are the sources of the list, not comparisons with a khipu. We know of no work that compares the column profile of a khipu with an ordered colonial list of pachacas.
- **Confidence:** low (no specific bibliographic search). To be asked of the specialist.

### I15. Pairs of columns according to the curacas (local lords) of Guancayo (Chillón, 1571)

- **Closest:** Espinoza Soriano 1963 (*Revista del Museo Nacional* XXXII; transcription of the *visita* of Martínez Rengifo) and Rostworowski, *Costa peruana prehispánica* (9 pachacas under 5 lords, 1 + 4×2).
- **Why it does not contain it:** they describe the organization and do not collate it with any khipu. Moreover, Espinoza attributes the *visita* to the Wanka of Huancayo, when the text concerns the Chillón (río de Collique, seven leagues to Los Reyes). On that point Rostworowski is right.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### I16–I17. Blind prediction of cords and test with anonymised data

- **Closest:**
  - Medrano and Khosla 2024 measure the prevalence of internal sums (74% of 650 khipus), not the prediction of hidden cords between khipus.
  - Clindaniel 2025 («Colorful Insights from an AI Khipukamayuq») describes the reciprocal arithmetic of the Inkawasi pairs, without predicting hidden cords and without baselines.
- **Why it does not contain it:** nobody has published a validation by omission of cords (leaving one out) nor a blind test with hidden cords, baselines and null controls on a khipu archive.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### I19. Copies UR273A≈UR274A and UR273B≈UR274B

- **Closest:** the 2015 supplement and the appendix of Urton 2017. They describe each khipu separately, and UR274A and UR274B only by their fixed value.
- **Why it does not contain it:** there is no comparison between UR273 and UR274.
- **Confidence:** high (for these sources).

### I20. Opposite attachment R/V (recto/verso) in the tied pairs (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton 2017, ch. 8 (`fuentes_txt/Urton2017_InkaHistoryInKnots.txt`, lines 1491–1524). In UR53B/UR53C the pair matches only if one is read from the recto and the other from the verso. Urton reads it as an opposition *hanan/hurin* (upper/lower moieties).
- **Why it does not contain it:** the phenomenon in one pair is published. What is ours is checking it in the three Inkawasi pairs and measuring it in the corpus: 6 of 14 pairs, p = 0.16. For that reason it remains as proposed.
- **Confidence:** high.

### I21. Error model and concrete cases for museum use

- **Closest:**
  - Thompson 2025 (corrections to the Ascher *databooks*, DOI 10.26188/30526775);
  - FitzPatrick 2026, «Knot Tricks» (topologically equivalent knot variants that are confused when transcribed);
  - Urton 2017, ch. 9: error correction between copies, 152 − 10 = 143 in UR267B, which UR256 corrects to 153.
- **Why it does not contain it:** none estimates error rates by type by comparing KFG with OKR, nor uses a null to decide whether a discrepancy is a reading error.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### I22. Three-level chain with two-way route (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton and Brezine 2005, and Urton 2017 ch. 5 (Puruchuco: «values knotted onto khipus sum upward through the hierarchy», p. 94); Urton 2017 ch. 4 (UR9 and UR21 synthesised in UR6).
- **Why it does not contain it:** the *type* of system is published. What is new is the concrete architecture of Inkawasi: the detail of 9 columns, table, book, and a second route through A/B pairs that reaches the same totals. We withdraw the expression «exclusive to Incahuasi».
- **Confidence:** high.

## Rest of the corpus

### C01–C02. The 74% is not informative; verifiable accounting in ~3%

- **Closest:** Medrano and Khosla 2024 (*Latin American Antiquity*, DOI 10.1017/laq.2024.5).
- **Why it does not contain it:**
  - they give the 74% as the prevalence of summing relations, without a null that shuffles within level and without estimating power;
  - their main argument was laterality, which we reproduce (54/46, p = 0.002, although half of it is explained by the position of the large values).
- **Recommendation:** present it as a methodological refinement of their work, not as a refutation. Our own state of the art had labelled it wrongly.
- **Confidence:** high.

### C04–C05. «Row plus loose total» format; first column larger

- **Closest:** Ascher and Ascher 1981 and the *Databook* (sum relations), and Medrano and Khosla 2024.
- **Why it does not contain it (as far as we know):** tabular formats are described khipu by khipu. We know of no test with a null of a «row plus loose total» format repeated across several khipus, nor of the «first column larger» archetype.
- **Confidence:** **low**, without a specific search.

### C06. Colour as a category with local key (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:**
  - Urton 2003 and 2017, and Medrano 2019, on colour as a context-dependent sign;
  - Morgallet 2026 (Zenodo, preprint): colour periodicity in 336 khipus;
  - Clindaniel 2019.
- **Why it does not contain it:** they propose or describe colour codes. We know of no contrast with a null of «colour → magnitude within the khipu» that survives shuffling within each group, together with the absence of shared vocabulary between sites.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C07. UR053A–E: stable W/R/AB distribution (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton 2017, ch. 8 (lines 1266–1524): the five khipus of the tied set share the sequence W/RL/AB, and UR53B/UR53C form a pair with a different format and opposite R/V. Urton proposes a paradigm of *ceques* (radial lines of shrines in Cusco). Of the other three he says: «Aside from color patterning, I did not find that the other three samples in the set represent the same kind of organization».
- **Why it does not contain it:** it does not measure the distribution of magnitudes among W, R and AB, nor its stability (close to 4:1:1) across the five khipus.
- **Confidence:** high for Urton 2017; low for other sources. We have not seen the Urton article in which he called the B/C pair «closely matching» (his note 12).

### C09. The positional zero measures a decimal band

- **Closest:**
  - Ascher and Ascher 1981: zero is the absence of a knot in a position;
  - Florio and Overmann 2025 (DOI 10.1163/15685373-12340204): they argue that the Inca system was not positional and had no zero;
  - Moscovich 2024.
- **Why it does not contain it:** nobody measures the physical gap of the zero against the width of the band. Our measurement contradicts Florio and Overmann, with the caveat that the assignment of value is partly circular.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C10. Canutos = fixing system

- **Closest:** Milillo 2024 (Zea Books, DOI 10.32873/unl.dc.zea.1616), with 9 Inca khipus whose wrappings are read as *kayte* (a Quechua term, here glossed as a subject marker); Hyland 2020.
- **Why it does not contain it:** Milillo is explicitly exploratory, with no counts and no statistics, and mentions the khipus with *canutos* only as post-Inca. He does not quantify the coverage (97.6%) nor the coincidence with the group boundaries.
- **Confidence:** medium. A specialist in textiles will ask for more khipus than UR052.

### C11. UR050: three beads per cord at fixed heights

- **Closest:** Mackey 1970, summarised in *Narrative Threads* 2002 (ch. 13): subtype B1-b, with several beads on one cord, in shepherds' khipus.
- **Why it does not contain it:** Mackey describes modern khipus; we know of no published analysis of UR050 with height zones.
- **Confidence:** low.

### C12. No mirror halves

- **Closest:** Urton 2003 and 2017 (duality in sign units and in pairs of khipus) and Pereyra 2006 (Armatambo, a mark that divides a cord into two sectors; a single case).
- **Why it does not contain it:** nobody had tested mirror symmetry at the scale of the corpus. Note: Urton does not claim that there are mirror halves, so the result must be formulated as «duality does not manifest as mirror symmetry», not as a refutation of Urton.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C13. Only 100 is overrepresented (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton 2017, ch. 12 (the pachaca as a round unit of 100), and Medrano and Urton 2018.
- **Why it does not contain it:** they do not establish which round values are overrepresented against a digit null. What is new is the negative result for 10, 15, 20 and 40, and that the «15» and the «47» of Inkawasi are due to the copies.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C14. Santa: the matching of census ↔ khipus does not survive the correct null

- **Closest:**
  - Medrano and Urton 2018 (descriptive alignment);
  - FitzPatrick 2024 (*Ethnohistory* 71(4)): 20 alignments and the best with p = 0.02985 uncorrected;
  - Contreras 2026 (arXiv 2607.00185): only the R/V ratio, and leaves the collation of the 367 weights «for future work».
- **Why it does not contain it:** nobody corrects for the 20 alignments (0.03 → 0.60) nor uses a null of compositions of 133 into 6.
- **Confidence:** high.

### C17. Calendar: general negative (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Urton 2017, ch. 4 (UR006 biennial) and Zuidema (ceques, *uncu* (a Quechua term for a garment)), in *Quipu y Tocapu*.
- **Why it does not contain it:** we know of no battery of calendrical tests with multiple-comparison correction across the whole corpus.
- **Confidence:** low.

### C18. Data records, not text; Sivan's syllabary fails

- **Closest:** Sivan 2026 (Zenodo, preprint, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19184002) and Clindaniel 2019 (grammar of the khipu).
- **Why it does not contain it:** Sivan proposes the syllabic channel, but does not test his own predictions (order effect, repetition) nor notices that his four key khipus belong to the family of *canutos*.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C19–C20. No detectable syllabic reading in the non-numerical ones; negative colonial templates

- **Closest:**
  - Hyland 2017 (Collata, logosyllabic reading);
  - Prescott 2026 (Zenodo): classification of 593 khipus by documentary type, without nulls;
  - Pärssinen and Kiviharju 2004: the documentary templates.
- **Why it does not contain it:** we know of no decipherment attempts with a positive control (real Quechua encrypted at random), nor of sweeps of colonial genres with confirmation halves.
- **Confidence:** low–medium.

### C21. Ceques UR050 + UR055, refuted

- **Closest:** Urton 2017, proposal of ceques in a pair of khipus (lines 1518–1524). He puts it forward for UR53B/C, not for UR050/UR055.
- **Why it does not contain it:** the hypothesis UR050 + UR055 is ours, and we refuted it ourselves.

### C23. No ancient khipu has the Collata inventory

- **Closest:** Hyland 2017 (*Current Anthropology*): 95 signs in the Collata epistles.
- **Why it does not contain it:** Hyland does not compare his inventory with the ancient corpus. The comparison is ours, and it leaves out fibre, which the ancient corpus does not record reliably.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C24. Copies between museums

- **Closest:**
  - Urton 2005 (*Khipu Archives*): AS194/AS195, AS159/AS173, AS114/AS123, AS116/AS150, Puruchuco and UR006/UR009/UR021;
  - Thompson 2024 (*Ñawpa Pacha*): numerical connection between two khipus from Arica.
- **Why it does not contain it:** none of the sources we know lists HP055 ≈ UR1084, AS038/KH0049 ≈ UR122 (partly; see K01), Basel ≈ UR212, UR1175 ≈ UR233 + UR232, UR1118 ≈ UR218 nor QU002 ≈ UR137.
- **Confidence:** **low**, without a specific search. This is the point where the specialist's knowledge of collections weighs most.

### C25. Hierarchy of copy fidelity (in whatever part is new)

- **Closest:** Medrano 2019 (thesis): «there exist khipus which are numerical copies of each other … which do not share the same colors, or even relative ordering of colors»; Urton 2005; Thompson 2024 (in Arica, colour does form part of the connection).
- **Why it does not contain it:** the qualitative idea is published. What is ours is the quantification against the re-reading of the same object.
- **Confidence:** medium.

### C26. Errors in the databases

- **Closest:**
  - Brezine, Clindaniel and Ghezzi 2024 (KH#### convention, DOI 10.1017/laq.2023.71);
  - Thompson 2025 and 2026 (corrections to the *databooks* and to the Peabody museum numbers);
  - Sánchez 2026 (Zenodo): recovery of the KDB, with KH0701 absent;
  - GitHub issues for OKR and KFG.
- **Why it does not contain it:** none documents the displacement of KH0197, KH0227, KH0267 and KH0350, the top and marker cords lost in KFG, AS081, the 38% of the *Databook* that is absent, nor the museum number of KH0676. Searches for those identifiers on GitHub give 0 results.
- **Confidence:** high.

### C28. Rosetta with colonial transcriptions: negative with power

- **Closest:** Medrano 2019 (thesis: 72 transcriptions, without complete lists of figures) and Pärssinen and Kiviharju 2004.
- **Why it does not contain it:** we know of no systematic collation of colonial sequences of figures against the whole corpus with a positive control.
- **Confidence:** low.

### C29. «Assignment versus count» signature

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2018 (fixed values) and the literature on the pachaca as a round unit (Urton 2017, ch. 12).
- **Why it does not contain it:** we know of no test of heterogeneity of roundness by khipu.
- **Confidence:** low.

### K01. KH0049 as a three-level hierarchical table; UR122 as its partial copy

- **Closest:** the Khipu Field Guide (catalogue and the «Indexed Pendant Sums» page of KH0049) lists 15 indexed sums (g1p1 = g4p1 + g5p1 + g6p1 + g7p1 = 27,475, etc.) as the automatic output of the Ascher method. Ascher and Ascher 1978 (AS038) and Pereyra Sánchez 2011 (MNAAHP) may describe it; we have not read them. In Nazca khipus from other museums hierarchical sums are known (KH0263/UR028, KH0235/AS215).
- **Why it does not contain it:** what we found does not describe the hierarchy in blocks of 18, does not contrast it with a null, and does not mention UR122 nor the discrepancy 15652/15649. KFG marks KH0049 and KH0362 (UR122) as «similar» without saying that they are a copy.
- **Confidence:** medium–low. Detail in `analisis/30_frontera_estructura/novedad_kh0049.md`.

### K02. Alternation of two colours A/B/A/B (UR040, UR167, UR1033F)

- **Closest:** serial colour khipus from the Santa (FitzPatrick 2024) and the general description of colour alternation in Ascher and Ascher; we have not checked whether they treat these khipus.
- **Why it does not contain it:** the null test that preserves runs and the replication in the confirmation half are ours. What is asserted is the colour structure, not a content.
- **Confidence:** low, without a specific search.

### K03. UR1104 → UR234 («extract from a field»), UR108 → UR231 and the copy as an archive trait

- **Closest:** Urton 2005 (*Khipu Archives*) and Thompson 2024 (Arica) describe copies and numerical connections between khipus in different museums.
- **Why it does not contain it:** neither lists UR1104 ≈ UR234 nor the type «a field that rises a level». The odds ratio of 17.9 against the pairs from other sites is our calculation.
- **Confidence:** low (grep and web search).

### K04. Lost book α and mismatches of UR278 as a turn-counting error

- **Closest:** Urton and Chu 2015 and 2018 document the copies UR266 ≈ UR275 and the levies; we have not seen a discussion of the cell-by-cell mismatches.
- **Why it does not contain it:** the analysis of the 7 mismatches and its inference of a lost intermediate step are ours.
- **Confidence:** medium (subject to the general caveat on the unread Chu texts).

### K05. «The larger cord opens the group» (P5c)

- **Closest:** the orientation of the top cord and the totals (Locke 1927; Ascher and Ascher 1981).
- **Why it does not contain it:** as far as we know, nobody has measured how often the larger value comes first across the corpus.
- **Confidence:** low.

### K06. Nissen, Damerow and Englund 1993, *Archaic Bookkeeping* (comparison with Uruk)

We use it to separate parallels from differences; it is not the antecedent of any finding.

- **Confirmed parallels, with page numbers:** numerical systems per product (the same sign has different values depending on the system; pp. 25–28, 37), fixed format by type of transaction (p. 30), totals on the reverse (p. 20) and chains of documents «tested in few cases» (Jemdet Nasr balance 62a–c, pp. 74–75), similar to UR275, which reorders UR266.
- **Differences:** the two-route reconciliation is **not** documented in Uruk (in Incahuasi it remains as an own trait, proposed). The descending order on p. ~60 is Old Akkadian (c. 2250 BC), not proto-cuneiform. The totals on the reverse can exceed the sum, so a mismatch does not prove an error. The type deficit o-pe-ro is Old Sumerian and Ur III. The book gives no error rates, only cases.
- **Lesson from p. 25:** until 1983, proto-cuneiform mismatches were attributed to scribal «calculation errors» when they were unrecognised systems. For that reason we checked whether the mismatches of UR278 are systematic (sign, column, magnitude). Result: they are not, and 6 of 7 fall in the units; we read turn-counting errors, not a convention.
- **Confidence:** high for what is cited with page numbers; knowledge of Uruk and Pylos outside that book is prior and unverified.
